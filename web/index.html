Impact math counts CH4 + N2O as CO2e

Burning one field of paddy straw releases methane (CH4) and nitrous oxide (N2O). Counted as CO2-equivalent, those two gases are the climate footprint of a burn that a prevented-burn attestation actually avoids.

Straw CO2 itself is not counted

The CO2 in the straw is biogenic and short-cycle — the next crop reabsorbs it — so IPCC accounting excludes it. Counting it would inflate every claim; PlotLock follows the IPCC rule instead.

Plus PM2.5, the harm you can breathe

Stubble smoke is a major seasonal source of fine particulate matter (PM2.5) over northern India. It is reported alongside the gases, as a health impact, not folded into the CO2e number. All impact figures are labelled projections — no market statistics are invented.

NoBurn Burned Uncertain Duplicate — rejected FIRMS detection

The verdict rule: two signals, both required — a FIRMS/VIIRS active-fire detection inside the plot (confidence n/h) and a Sentinel-2 burn scar (mean dNBR ≥ 0.10, or ≥ 25% of pixels ≥ 0.15, USGS scale). Both clean → NoBurn. Both positive → Burned. A signal missing or the two disagreeing → Uncertain. Only NoBurn records a payout-eligible attestation; Uncertain never pays.

Select a plot on the map (or a card below it) to inspect its evidence bundle, verdict and on-chain record.

Honesty notes. Sentinel-2 item IDs, dates and cloud cover are real (Microsoft Planetary Computer STAC, tile T43RFP, Oct–Nov 2025). Plot 1 and plot 3 are fully measured. Plot 2 is partly mirrored: its item metadata and pre-fire NBR are real, but the post-fire scar metrics and FIRMS rows are mirrored to a typical low-severity stubble burn, because the real Oct–Nov 2025 analysis found no field with a persistent scar in the demo area and a live FIRMS pull needs a NASA MAP_KEY this project does not hold. Nothing mirrored is passed off as measured. No-detection is not proof of no-burn: FIRMS can miss short fires between overpasses — which is why both signals must agree before anything is attested.
